The Avatar in Chief

2037 · synthetic media · 6 min 20 sec

Synopsis

A synthetic newsmagazine segment from 2037. Campaigning and governing have merged into one interactive AI avatar of the US President. Leveraging parasocial relationships, it speaks with millions of voters to respond to their concerns like never before.

Director's Statement

The Avatar in Chief is a speculative news piece: a segment from Volume 70 of 60 Minutes, airing in 2037, in which a correspondent files a story on a U.S. president and the synthetic AI avatar she deployed first as a campaign tool, then as a mode of democratic representation. It uses the genre tropes of the prestige news-magazine format to dramatize one possible future — a world where AI is so normalized that its presence in the presidency is newsworthy, but only barely. While I was making it, the real 60 Minutes underwent its own crisis of legitimacy under new leadership; the trusted format I was forging was losing its trustworthiness in real time, which gave the project an unexpected urgency.

My goal was to explore a world after the legitimacy debate is over — where we have stopped arguing about whether these tools should exist and started mapping the futures they enable. The piece works by borrowing the credibility of a trusted format to smuggle in a radical change, normalized as barely newsworthy. To build it I mixed fully synthesized characters, settings, and scenes with real archival footage, and where the footage I needed didn't exist, I synthesized it — a recreation of an FDR speech that was delivered but never recorded, a lost Instagram Live that I rebuilt from screenshots. Even parts of the documentary past, here, are partly generative.

The work's disclosure relies on its future date to mark it as fiction. I had hoped the uncanny faces and upscaling artifacts might work as a second layer of disclosure — a generative sheen that plays into the argument — but experience tells me audiences forgive or simply fail to register those tells. I know that putting words in the mouths of public figures is a fraught position. I hope the debate the piece provokes justifies the borrowing of credibility it performs.

Biography

Lance Robertson is a poet, technologist, and critical futurist working in synthetic media and speculative design. His practice draws on backgrounds in spoken-word poetry, software development, and media theory. He hosts Neomania, a podcast of conversations on technology and the future, and studies speculative design at the University of California, San Diego.
